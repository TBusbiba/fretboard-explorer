Three combinations, then the keepers

Riso Zine, Bento Studio (richer now), Neo-Brutalist (round notes) and Studio Console (new neck). Every neck now follows one rule: two colours, the root in one that pops (bigger, ringed, glowing) and every other note in a second, calm one. All four also use the readable layout: real-guitar inlays, strings named and numbered and drawn to gauge, a clear nut, and a number under every fret.

C2Printed Hardware combination · light

Studio Console is the base: the app is a device, with screws, a display, knobs for root and scale, LED keys for the views, a practice slip with a mic meter, and a REC button. It's printed in Riso's two inks on paper: the display is a blue halftone panel with a pink-offset "C MAJ", and the LEDs are pink dots. Neo-Brutalist provides the construction, with ink outlines and hard shadows on every control. The neck is the console neck, set into the paper device.

02Bento Studio now richer

Same idea, a lot more going on. The tiles have depth and light, with icons and small mono labels. The key tile adds a mode switcher, the practice tile shows a live mic waveform, and the streak shows the days of the week. The neck tile gets its own view controls and legend, plus three new tiles: an interval ruler (steps drawn to scale), the C chord box, and a chord-map peek. A command-search pill and a streak ring sit in the header.

04Studio Console new neck

Same hardware idea: a warm grey device, green LCD, knobs for root and scale, rubber keys with LED dots, and a practice screen with a mic meter. The neck is built like Neo-Brutalist's (flat, crisp lines, round notes with a hard offset shadow) but in the console's palette. It sits in a matte black strip with warm-grey strings and frets, and small orange LED inlays. Every note is a flat panel key the colour of the device body, and the root is the one orange key: bigger, with a soft LED glow.
